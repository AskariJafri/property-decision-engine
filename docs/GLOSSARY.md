# Glossary

Every term this product uses, in plain language, with the ones that cost people
money flagged. Written for someone buying their first home in Ontario.

Figures are current as at 2026-09 and carry their effective dates in
`research/RESEARCH_REPORT.md` §3. Nothing here is advice.

---

## The five that trip people up

**Amortization is not the term.** The **amortization** is how long until the
mortgage is fully paid off — usually 25 years. The **term** is how long your
current contract and rate last — usually 5 years. At the end of the term you
renew at whatever rates exist then, with maybe 20 years still to run. People hear
"5-year mortgage" and think they own the house in five years. They don't.

**Mortgage insurance protects the lender, not you.** CMHC mortgage default
insurance pays *the bank* if you can't. You pay the premium; the bank gets the
protection. It is not life insurance and it does not help your family if something
happens to you. It is the price of being allowed to buy with less than 20% down.

**Land transfer tax and property tax are different things.** Land transfer tax is
paid **once**, at closing, when the title changes hands. Property tax is paid
**every year**, forever, to the municipality. Toronto charges land transfer tax
twice — once provincially, once municipally.

**"Approved" is not "affordable."** A lender tests whether your ratios clear
published limits. That is a different question from whether you can live on what's
left. This product keeps them apart deliberately.

**The $1.5M cliff.** Mortgage insurance is unavailable above a $1,500,000 purchase
price. At $1.5M you can put 10% down ($125,000). At $1,500,001 you must put 20%
down ($300,000). One dollar of price, $175,000 more cash.

## Money at the start

**Asking price / list price** — what the seller is advertising. Not what it is
worth and not necessarily what it sells for.

**Down payment** — the cash you put in. The legal minimum in Canada is tiered:
5% of the first $500,000, 10% of the portion from $500,000 to $1.5M, and 20% of
the whole price above $1.5M. On $850,000 that is $55,000 minimum.

**Principal** — the amount you actually borrow. Price minus down payment, plus the
insurance premium if it is financed.

**Loan-to-value (LTV)** — the mortgage as a percentage of the price. Put 10% down
and your LTV is 90%. It sets your insurance premium rate.

**Closing costs** — everything else due on closing day: land transfer taxes, legal
fees, title insurance, inspection, adjustments. Budget 2–4% of the price. On an
$850,000 Toronto purchase this product computes about $24,600.

**Cash to close** — down payment plus closing costs. The number that actually has
to be in your account.

## The mortgage

**Interest rate (contract rate)** — the rate you are actually charged. Distinct
from the *posted* rate, which is a sticker price almost nobody pays, and from the
*qualifying* rate used to test you.

**Fixed vs variable** — fixed stays put for the term; variable moves with the
lender's prime rate. Fixed is a bet that rates rise; variable is a bet they fall.

**Semi-annual compounding** — a Canadian quirk. Fixed mortgages compound twice a
year, not monthly, so the monthly payment is slightly lower than a US-style
calculator says. $500,000 at 5% over 25 years is **$2,908.02** here; the US
convention would say $2,922.95. Any Canadian calculator that disagrees is wrong.

**Mortgage default insurance (CMHC insurance)** — mandatory with less than 20%
down. The premium is a percentage of the loan, by LTV: 2.80% at 85%, 3.10% at 90%,
4.00% at 95%. It is normally added to your mortgage and paid off over the
amortization, so **you also pay interest on it**.

**Insured vs uninsured** — insured means less than 20% down and a premium.
Uninsured means 20% or more down, no premium, and different qualifying rules.

**30-year amortization** — only available on an insured mortgage if you are a
first-time buyer or buying a new build. Lower payment, more total interest.

## Qualifying

**Stress test (minimum qualifying rate, MQR)** — you must be able to afford the
payment at the **greater of 5.25% or your contract rate plus 2%**. At 4.09% you
are tested at 6.09%. You are not charged that rate; you just have to survive it on
paper.

**Gross debt service (GDS)** — housing costs (mortgage + property tax + heat + half
the condo fee) as a share of gross income. The guideline is **39%**.

**Total debt service (TDS)** — GDS plus all your other debt payments: car loan,
credit cards, student loans, support payments. The guideline is **44%**. This is
usually what actually stops people.

**Pre-approval vs approval** — a pre-approval is a rate hold and an estimate. Real
approval comes after the lender sees the specific property and the paperwork. A
pre-approval can and does fall through.

## Taxes and one-time costs

**Land transfer tax (LTT)** — provincial, paid once at closing, on a sliding scale.
On $850,000 in Ontario: $13,475.

**Municipal land transfer tax (MLTT)** — Toronto only, and roughly doubles the
above. Since 1 April 2026 it also has luxury bands running to 8.60% above $20M.
A $4M house pays $19,000 more than it would have in March.

**First-time buyer rebates** — up to **$4,000** provincially and up to **$4,475**
in Toronto. Both are capped, so on a cheap purchase you get only what you owed.
"First-time" means never having owned a home anywhere in the world.

**Non-resident speculation tax (NRST)** — 25% across Ontario for foreign buyers,
plus another 10% in Toronto. On an $850,000 purchase that is $297,500. This
product asks about residency rather than assuming.

**Title insurance** — a one-off policy against defects in ownership: fraud, survey
errors, unregistered liens. Usually a few hundred dollars.

**Status certificate** — condos only. A package describing the corporation's
finances, rules and any lawsuits. Your lawyer reads it. It is where you find out
the reserve fund is thin or a special assessment is coming.

**Adjustments** — settling up on closing day for things the seller prepaid, such as
property tax already paid for the rest of the year.

## Costs that never stop

**Monthly ownership cost** — the real number: mortgage + property tax ÷ 12 +
insurance ÷ 12 + condo fee + utilities + maintenance reserve. Most calculators show
you only the mortgage, which is how people end up house-poor while every tool told
them they were fine.

**Property tax** — annual, set by your municipality as a percentage of assessed
value. Rates vary more than people expect: Toronto 0.7673%, Mississauga 1.0339%,
Ottawa 1.2271%. On the same $850,000 house that is a difference of over $380 a
month.

**Condo fee (maintenance fee)** — monthly, covers building operations and the
reserve fund. Usually the largest cost after the mortgage. A suspiciously low fee
often means an underfunded reserve and a special assessment later.

**Special assessment** — a one-off bill from a condo corporation when the reserve
fund cannot cover a major repair. Can be tens of thousands of dollars.

**Maintenance reserve** — money you set aside yourself for a freehold home. About
1% of value a year is the convention. Not a bill, but not optional either.

## Programs that give you money

**FHSA (First Home Savings Account)** — $8,000 a year, $40,000 lifetime.
Contributions are deductible like an RRSP and withdrawals for a first home are
tax-free like a TFSA. The best of both, and the first place most first-time buyers
should put money.

**Home Buyers' Plan (HBP)** — borrow up to $60,000 from your own RRSP for a first
home, repaid over 15 years. Miss a repayment and that year's share becomes taxable
income.

You can use both for the same purchase.

## What this product computes

**Fair value range** — an estimate expressed as a range, never a single number.
"$820,000–$845,000" is honest; "$832,451" is false precision.

**Comparables ("comps")** — recent sales of similar nearby properties. The basis of
any real valuation. This product cannot fetch them, so you supply them, and the
range narrows as you add more.

**Buy Score** — a 0–100 composite of eight components, weighted by what you said
matters. Withheld entirely if more than 35% of the weighting could not be computed.

**Confidence** — how much evidence sits behind a number, separate from the number
itself. A score of 85 at 45% confidence and a score of 85 at 90% confidence are
different claims.

**Reserve months** — how many months of ownership cost you have left *after*
closing. Two months is thin. Six is comfortable.

## Investment terms

**Rental yield / cap rate** — annual net operating income divided by price. Ignores
the mortgage, so it compares properties rather than deals.

**Cash flow** — rent minus *everything*, including the mortgage. Negative cash flow
means the property costs you money every month.

**Cash-on-cash return** — annual cash flow divided by the cash you put in. The
number that tells you what your money is actually earning.

**Vacancy allowance** — the share of the year you assume it sits empty. Assuming
zero is the most common mistake in amateur rental maths.

**House hack** — living in part of a property and renting the rest, such as a
basement suite. Changes both the affordability and the tax picture.
